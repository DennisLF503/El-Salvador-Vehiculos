Car glossary

General explanations of terms you will see on vehicle pages and when buying. For the exact figures of each model, check the manufacturer's technical sheet.

4x2 and 4x4 drive
Shows how many wheels receive power from the engine. 4x2 drives two wheels and is the most common setup in the city. 4x4 shares power among all four wheels and helps on dirt, mud or steep hills.
AWD (all-wheel drive)
Shares power among all four wheels automatically. It is common in SUVs and sedans, and helps in rain and on slippery surfaces.
Manual transmission
You change gears with the clutch pedal and the gear lever. It takes practice in heavy traffic.
Automatic transmission
The vehicle changes gears by itself. It is usually more comfortable in the city.
CVT
A continuously variable transmission: it has no fixed gears, but a ratio that adjusts smoothly. It is common in small and mid-size cars.
Diesel and gasoline engines
Diesel usually delivers good pulling force at low engine speeds, which suits heavy loads. Gasoline is usually smoother and is common in city cars.
Engine displacement
The total volume of the engine cylinders. It is measured in cubic centimeters (cc) or liters. On its own, it does not tell you how efficient or powerful an engine is.
Torque
The turning force the engine produces. You feel it when starting off, climbing a hill or pulling a load.
Horsepower
How quickly the engine can deliver that force. You feel it when accelerating at higher speeds.
Ladder frame
A structure with two long beams running along the vehicle. It is common in pickups and trucks, and handles cargo and towing well.
Unibody (monocoque)
The body and the structure form a single piece. It is common in sedans and SUVs, and usually gives more comfortable handling in the city.
Ground clearance
The distance between the ground and the lowest part of the vehicle. More clearance helps over potholes, rocks and mud.
Payload capacity
The maximum weight the vehicle can carry, including people and cargo. This figure is in the manufacturer's technical sheet and must not be exceeded.
ABS
Stops the wheels from locking under hard braking, so you can keep steering.
Electronic stability control (ESC)
Helps keep the vehicle on its path in corners or when it starts to slide.
Chassis number (VIN) and engine number
They identify the vehicle. They should match the documents before you buy.
Tire pressure
The recommended air pressure for the tires. Check it when the tires are cold. The value is usually on a label inside the door or in the manual.
